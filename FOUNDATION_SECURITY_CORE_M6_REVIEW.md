Owner is an employee-realm administrative assignment, never an ID-based bypass. Service actor distinct; delegated subject, delegation ID, audience and scope explicit when applicable. - Immutable server-derived command: tenant/environment, actor identity, delegated subject where applicable, exact operation-effect set with resource type/stable ID/location/field scope, normalized payload, expected revision and command key. Fingerprint includes all authorization-relevant immutable command facts; correlation and tool name are not identity. Do not include expiring session proof or policy revision in idempotency identity; fresh decisions on replay still mandatory. Distinct targets, actors or effects conflict; reject ambiguous/missing scope and inconsistent effect-to-target binding. Canonical ordering must not erase semantically different effects. Domain owns effect derivation and targets, Core owns fingerprint.
- Typed locks {type,id} acceptable test-only if tenant isolated by fixture and stable key, including absent targets. Lock order deterministic; revisions and uniqueness checked from locked state. After lock acquisition rederive or verify exact effects from current records; any changed effect/resource/revision invalidates original command and approval, no silent expansion.
- Check all exact effects before claim, after claim, after locks before apply, immediately before commit, before disclosure and reconciliation. Each check receives same immutable command plus stage and current authoritative evidence. Failure/error/timeout/unknown denies. For synthetic M6 literal true must be from trusted injected adapter only; do not allow truthy objects, browser flags, unknown aliases, or stale cached allow. No automatic grant based on workflow/tool access.
- Precommit denial rolls back local effects, receipt, transactional audit/outbox. Denial audit may be separate, append-only and minimal. After commit denial or projection failure must not imply rollback or permit automatic retry; return safe non-disclosing committed/unknown outcome per Core contract, no unauthorized receipt. Distinguish outcome classification from access to outcome details. Reconciliation inspection requires separately scoped authority and original actor/effect binding or explicit reviewed reconciliation workflow. No automatic replay after unknown commit.
- Replay must never reapply effects. Require current underlying execution grants and independent current read/field authorization before projection; no unrestricted receipt access. Minimal receipt allowlist only IDs/revisions/outcome identifiers; even IDs may require scoped disclosure. Reprojection must not return customer notes, costs or other fields without current permitted read projection. Ordinary authorized employee cost and customer contact/notes/financial read defaults retained, restrictions override. No raw payment credentials.
- Approval is separate from authentication and authorization; bind approval to actor/request revision, exact targets/effects, approved amount where relevant, expiry and reviewer. Revalidate reviewer authority, approval status, and current executor grants where policy requires. Independent reviewer for designated high-risk actions, explicit controlled Owner exception only; thresholds, exception conditions not frozen. Test fixtures may represent approved/denied/expired/revoked/mismatched approval facts, not an approved runtime approval protocol.
- On-site valid PIN session with trusted site/device requires no per-action PIN/MFA for authorized ordinary business actions (refund, product create, credit, cost etc). Security admin requires recent strong proof; freshness maximum pending. - Outbox records are local intent, not delivery authorization. Delivery/retry requires current scoped service authority, applicable delegation and consent/effects; no reuse of human PIN/session. No external/vendor side effects inside local transaction. Catalog remote mutation seam separate; no blind retry of uncertain vendor write.

## Revocation and commit boundary
A precommit check narrows the race but does not make distributed revocation linearizable. Do not claim atomic revocation based on M6 tests. Future production architecture must define the authorization/commit linearization point, shared policy epoch or transactional fencing/locking where technically possible, and behavior when Security and business storage cannot share a transaction. A signed short-lived decision alone is not a revocation fence. If a protected operation cannot meet its approved assurance, fail closed; explicitly document already-committed effects versus future denied operations. Test-only PG may simulate a policy epoch/fence, not imply production atomicity.
